### Research Focus: Adversarial Robustness in Cooperative Multi-Agent Learning

In cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL), autonomous agents learn to coordinate in shared environments where individual decisions affect the entire group. When observations are deliberately manipulated, consequences need not remain confined to the targeted agent: a robot receiving corrupted sensor information may take inappropriate actions that force peers to react, either containing the disturbance or compounding its damage.

My research investigates the relationship between **individual vulnerability and collective performance** in cooperative teams:

* **Individual vs. Collective Consequences:** Analyzing how localized observation perturbations propagate through cooperative teams, and characterizing whether peer responses represent harmful disruption or useful compensation.
* **Team Organization (Heterogeneity & Communication):** Examining how differences in agents' learned behaviors (**behavioral heterogeneity**) and **inter-agent communication** influence team robustness, without assuming diversity alone guarantees resilience.
* **Adaptive Execution-Time Protection:** Developing defense mechanisms that operate during execution while **preserving pretrained policy parameters**. This line of inquiry explores principles of differentiated collective protection—such as artificial immune systems—with **multi-robot teams** as a prospective empirical direction.
